# Structural & Formatting Artifacts

## The sandwich (restatement bookends)

AI text opens by restating the topic and closes by summarizing what was just said. In short-to-medium content, both are unnecessary.

**Pattern:** "Authentication is a critical part of any web application. In this article, we'll explore..." → [body] → "In summary, authentication is a critical part of any web application and..."

**Fix:** Start with your first real point. End when you're done making points.

## Over-scoping

Answering a narrow question with a comprehensive overview of the entire domain. Someone asks about a specific edge case and gets a 1,500-word primer from first principles.

**Fix:** Match scope of response to scope of question. Go deep on the specific thing, not wide on everything adjacent.

## Excessive signposting

Announcing what you're about to do instead of doing it.

**Flagged phrases:** "Let's explore this further," "Now let's turn our attention to," "Let's break this down," "With that context established, we can now look at," "Before we get into X, it's important to understand Y"

**Fix:** Delete the signpost. Start the next section. The heading is the signpost.

## The unprompted recap

A "To summarize" or "In conclusion" section that restates the body.

**Rule:** Under ~2,000 words, you don't need a recap. In longer pieces, a conclusion must add something — a recommendation, a next step, an opinion — not restate the body.

## Tricolon addiction

AI defaults to groups of three. "It's fast, reliable, and scalable."

**Fix:** Use the actual number of items that exist. Sometimes two. Sometimes five. Sometimes one thing that matters.

## Symmetrical parallelism in lists

Every bullet starts with the same part of speech, same length, same internal structure.

**Diagnostic:** If you could swap any two bullets without the reader noticing, the list is too uniform.

**Fix:** Let items vary in length and structure. Some fragments, some multi-sentence. Break the rhythmic pattern.

## Formatting overuse

Bold on every third phrase. Headers for two-sentence sections. Bullet points where a paragraph reads better. Numbered lists for things with no meaningful order.

**Rule:** Minimum formatting that makes content scannable. Three-sentence section doesn't need a header. Two-item list goes inline.
